Understanding Karet (“Cut Off”)

Appearing over thirty times across the Pentateuch, the sanction karet (“cutting off” or “excision”) applies to sacral or hidden covenantal breaches such as consuming blood, eating chametz at Passover, neglecting circumcision, or defiling holy offerings.

  • Punitive Expulsion (G. Thomas Hobson, 2010): Traces karet from fifth-century BCE Jewish practice to Josephus and Qumran as community expulsion—a merciful alternative to execution that removes ritual contamination, paralleling Hittite hurkel and Mesopotamian uzug expulsion.
  • Divine Sanction (Milgrom & Rabbinic Tradition): Views karet as an act executed directly by God—premature death before age 50 or 60 (Moed Katan 28a), loss of descendants (Rashi), or exclusion of the soul from the World to Come (Maimonides, Nachmanides)—especially where human courts lack eyewitnesses.
  • Dual Enforcement: For overt acts like public Sabbath breach or Molech worship, human courts impose stoning (Sekilah) whilst divine karet stands if the community fails to prosecute (Lev 20:2–3; Exod 31:14).
